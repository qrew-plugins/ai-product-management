# Usability bank: ten heuristics (pinned)

Source: Jakob Nielsen, "10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design", Nielsen Norman Group, first published 1994, last reviewed 2024-01-30: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/ . Names checked against the source on 2026-10-26. Descriptions below are our own plain-language summaries for auditing.

Cite as `heuristic: <name> (#n)`. A heuristic finding is `judgment` evidence unless a measurement backs it, so it is never above Medium on its own.

| # | Heuristic | What to look for in a dashboard, report or app |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Visibility of System Status | Users can tell what's loading, when data was last refreshed, whether a save worked |
| 2 | Match Between the System and the Real World | Labels use the team's words (from the glossary), numbers use their units and periods |
| 3 | User Control and Freedom | Undo, cancel and back are available; filters can be cleared |
| 4 | Consistency and Standards | The same thing is named, colored and placed the same way everywhere |
| 5 | Error Prevention | Risky actions confirm first; inputs prevent impossible values |
| 6 | Recognition Rather than Recall | Context stays visible; users don't have to remember a value from another screen |
| 7 | Flexibility and Efficiency of Use | Frequent jobs are quick; shortcuts exist for repeat users |
| 8 | Aesthetic and Minimalist Design | The main number or action stands out; nothing competes with it |
| 9 | Help Users Recognize, Diagnose, and Recover from Errors | Errors say what went wrong and what to do, in plain words |
| 10 | Help and Documentation | Where a job is complex, help sits next to it |
